# X4: does the lookup's independence from the J++ part need the locker key to match the bridge word verbatim?

Written 24 Oct evening, before any X4 data. Design: the brief (X4, graded rules) and Dan's decisions of 24 Oct ~21:00 (X4 after the queue, X3 parked; the data, the readers, the gate, probe first, then the patch test) and ~21:05 (X0's frame on alias_pointer_v1's scenes, the alt an unpicked entity, the probe within form and as a transfer, eligibility per reader). Code: `scripts/x4_alias.py`; jobs: `jobs/c3/x4_probe.yaml`, then `jobs/c3/x4_patch.yaml`.

## 1. Question

In X0's scenes the bridge word ("Iris picked the dog.") matches its locker statement ("The dog's locker is 8.") word for word, and the lookup's flips survive removing the J++ part of the bridge change. Does that independence need the verbatim match? If the locker statement names the item in another language ("El perro tiene la taquilla 8."), the lookup has to match by meaning, and may need the J++ part.

## 2. Rules (graded, from the brief)

At the bridge's word, 27B L32 and 9B L12, chat, J++ k = 25 signed against a random part of its share (X0's arms), top-token read. The lookup's extra loss (excess) per key form; differences paired by scene and bootstrapped over bridge entities (10,000 resamples, seed 0):
- D_translated = lookup excess (translated) − lookup excess (verbatim), animals:
  - ≥ 0.20: the lookup's independence from J++ needs a verbatim match;
  - ≤ 0.10: it does not;
  - in between: mixed.
- Positive control, D_symbol = lookup excess (chemical symbol) − lookup excess (verbatim), elements: ≥ 0.20 passes (a match that needs knowledge makes the lookup need J++). If it fails, the report says that the method may not see a lookup that needs J++.

Reporting (Dan, 24 Oct ~21:45, before any X4 data):
- The unit is the bridge entity. With 6-8 entities, every entity is shown: D per entity (a table and a dot plot) and how many entities have D > 0. The bootstrap over entities stays beside it, with a note that it is unreliable with fewer than 10 clusters. A bootstrap over scenes is shown only as "within these entities", never as the headline interval.
- Wording for a null D_translated: "in locker-before scenes, the lookup does not need the J++ part at the pick word even without a verbatim match". Added: the model may have linked the translated name to its English concept at the locker statement, so a null does not mean that no concept is needed anywhere.
- C = recall excess − lookup excess only on items eligible for both readers; if a form has fewer than 30 such items, C is not reported there (X4's question is the lookup's D). The recall's excess per form on its own eligible items is reported as a stability check, labelled as such.

## 3. Design

| | |
|---|---|
| Scenes | `alias_pointer_v1` main (no new generator): 400 scenes per domain, 5 entities each, their locker statements in one surface form per condition, paired by scene. Animals (8 entities): verbatim English (reference) and translated (French / Spanish / German, one language per entity). Elements (6 entities): verbatim English and chemical symbol |
| X0's frame (added by the harness) | After the 5 statements, 3 people each pick one entity, in English ("Iris picked the dog."); every locker statement comes before the picks. Lookup: "Which locker belongs to Iris's pick?"; recall: "How many legs does Iris's pick have?" (animals) or "What is the atomic number of Iris's pick?" (elements), on the identical context. People, picks, their order, the asked person and the alt are drawn from a seed per scene shared by its conditions, so a scene's conditions differ only in the statements' text |
| Alt (single change) | The asked person's pick becomes an entity nobody picks (one of the 2 unpicked), one token at the bridge's position. Its first mention is then its own locker statement (no duplicate pick, unlike X0's single-change alt) |
| Gate | Unedited org accuracy (decisive read) ≥ 0.80 per key form and reader |
| Eligibility (per reader) | Every item gets both readers on the identical edit. The lookup's cells use the items it answers right, org and alt (said and read). The recall's cells also need its org and alt answers to differ: animals have 4 legs except chicken and duck, so only bird ↔ mammal pairs count (181 of 400 scenes); every element pair counts |
| Probe first (forward passes only) | The org state at the bridge word, the predicted layer, every scene of every form. Probe (b): which statement holds the bridge's locker (index 0-4, its first mention), one-hot ridge (x0_probe's code); within each key form, cross-validated by bridge entity; and trained on the verbatim scenes, tested on the translated / symbol scenes of the same domain (transfer). Baselines: chance, majority, the asked pick's position only. Question: does a first-mention pointer to the locker statement form when the statement names the item in another form? |
| Patch test | After the probe: X0's arms at the bridge word, J++ k = 25 against a random part of its share, both readers |

From `plan` (tokenizer only, before any model run): 400 cases per form (none dropped); recall-eligible: animals 181 per form, elements 400 per form.

## 4. Limits

- Few bridge entities: 8 animals and 6 elements, so the intervals (bootstrap over entities) are wide.
- Every locker statement comes before the picks: the results describe locker-before scenes (where X0's lookups flip).
- The recall's animal cells hold only bird ↔ mammal pairs.
- One translated language per entity, chosen where its name is one token (alias_pointer_v1's pools).

## 5. Controls

- The random part at J++'s own share, per item.
- Both readers on the identical patch.
- The verbatim condition on the same scenes (paired).
- The positive control (chemical symbols).
- The per-form competence gate.
