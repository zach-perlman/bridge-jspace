## Question

C3 (post hoc, 23 Oct): past a model-specific depth, removing the J++-readable part of a stated bridge's edit stops hurting lookups and keeps hurting recalls. X0 asks three things:
1. Does that hold at a depth fixed in advance, on fresh scenes?
2. Does it hold in raw text as well as with the chat template?
3. Is it specific to J++, or would any token-aligned dictionary do (W_U, W_E)?

**Edit.** org = `context`. alt = `cf_context`, the generator's counterfactual: it swaps the asked person's pick with another person's. Both are token-aligned (`tasks_v2.prepare`). Only the asked person's bridge word is patched. In the alt prompt the other person's pick also changes. Where that sentence comes before the asked one, Δ at the asked word carries that upstream change too. The share of such items is reported, and the verdict is repeated on the asked-first subset (descriptive). **DECIDE (D5)**: use the cf as is (as on 23 Oct; recommended) or build a single-change alt.

**Eligibility** (per model and rendering):
- The 23 Oct competence filter (`tasks_v2.eligible`, level 0): PP and PM right on every prompt of the scene (whole question, each step, C, O). This covers the brief's condition that org and alt are answered right by both readers.
- `tasks_v2.prepare`'s first-token check: each reader's alt answer differs from its org answer.
- Single-token bridges: the `bridge_word` position set has exactly one token, in the org prompt and in the alt prompt.

Items are the first 200 eligible scenes in scene-id order. Fewer than 150 means "can't test (n)". Counts dropped at each filter are reported.

## Decomposition and arms

At layer L and the bridge's word: Δ = h_alt − h_org, the residual-stream output of block L, one row.

**Dictionaries.** All four use the same atoms: the target's letter-bearing tokens (`model.letter_token_ids()`, as on 23 Oct), unit rows.
- **J++** (main arm): `JLens(model, pins.FROZEN_LENS).vectors(L, letters)` = unit((W_U[t] ⊙ g) · J_L).
- **W_U** (control): unit(W_U[t] ⊙ g), the same with J = identity. This is Zach's `unembed` dictionary.
- **W_E** (control): unit(embed_tokens[t]), the input embeddings.
- **random** (control): unit Gaussian rows, seed 777 + L. This is the 23 Oct `rand_omp` dictionary.

**Fit.** D(Δ) = the k = 25 atom OMP fit of Δ over D. **DECIDE (D1)**: signed OMP, as on 23 Oct (`exp04_brew_parts.signed_omp`), or non-negative OMP, as the brief's section 2 writes. Recommended: signed is the main arm, because the rule's thresholds were set on it. Non-negative J++ would be an extra arm at the predicted layer only (descriptive).

**Random part, per dictionary.** Rand_D(Δ) projects Δ onto a random subspace (basis seed 888 + L) and keeps D(Δ)'s share of ‖Δ‖². This is `statement_positions.random_subspace_part`. Each dictionary gets its own size-matched random arm.

**Arms on the org run** (one forward each; all arms of an item and layer batched):
- whole: h_org + Δ;
- for each D: no_D = h_org + Δ − D(Δ), and no_rand_D = h_org + Δ − Rand_D(Δ).

That is 9 arms per item, reader and layer.

**At the predicted layer only**, the k curve: J++ and W_E at k = 10, 50 and 100, each with its no_D and no_rand_D arms. That adds 12 arms.

**Readers.** PP (lookup) and PM (recall) read the identical patched context. The question comes after the patched position, so the edit is the same for both.

## Read

Switch = the alt answer's first token ranks first among the relation's candidate first tokens. On these sets every answer is a number, so the candidates are the digit tokens 0-9. That is the brief's cand rule.

Also logged from the same forward pass: the 23 Oct read (the top token over the full vocabulary is the alt answer's), p(org), p(alt), and logit(alt) − logit(org). The two reads' agreement is reported. **DECIDE (D2)**: the cand read decides (recommended, because raw prompts need it). With the chat template the two reads coincide whenever the top token is a digit.

Ten random rows per cell (seed 0) also generate 8 greedy tokens for each of whole, no_J++ and no_rand_J++, as `gen_spot.py` does.

## Metrics, per model, rendering, reader, layer, dictionary

- S_x = share of items switched by arm x.
- Jdep_D = 1 − S_noD / S_whole.
- Rdep_D = 1 − S_noRandD / S_whole.
- excess_D = Jdep_D − Rdep_D.
- share_D = the median and mean over items of ‖D(Δ)‖² / ‖Δ‖².

**CIs.** 95% percentile bootstrap over the org bridge entity: entities are resampled with replacement with all their items, 10,000 resamples, seed 0. The ratios are recomputed per resample. Single rates get Wilson intervals.

**Valid cell.** S_whole ≥ 0.30 and at least 30 items switched by the whole edit, for that reader. Otherwise the cell is "can't tell". An invalid predicted cell is reported as "can't tell"; no other position or layer stands in for it.

**Labels** (section 2 of the brief):
- around: excess ≤ 0.10 and its upper bound ≤ 0.20;
- through: Jdep ≥ 0.30 and excess ≥ 0.20 with lower bound ≥ 0.10;
- else mixed.

**Split at L**: the lookup is "around" and the recall "through" at L, on the same items. The split is reported per layer for every model (descriptive). Only the predicted layer decides.

## Decision rule (the README rule of 23 Oct, unchanged)

On the **primary set, chat rendering, J++ dictionary, at the predicted layer** (27B L32 and 9B L12, at the word), per model, on point estimates:
- **confirmed** iff lookup Jdep ≤ lookup Rdep + 0.10 (lookup excess ≤ 0.10) **and** recall Jdep ≥ 0.30;
- **killed** (C3 not confirmed) iff lookup excess ≥ 0.20 **or** recall Jdep < 0.20;
- **not confirmed** otherwise;
- **can't tell** if either reader's predicted cell is invalid.

The labels are reported beside the rule.

**DECIDE (D3)**:
- (a) "Not confirmed, not killed" is a zone the brief's stop rule doesn't cover. Recommended: the README says "not confirmed", and X1-X4 run only where the lookup is labeled "around" at the predicted layer.
- (b) The verdict is per model. Recommended: the stop rule acts per model (a killed model leaves X1-X4), and it is a full stop if both are killed.
- (c) The 27B lookup at the word fades with depth (84 / 72 / 63 / 48% at L16-28), so L32 may be "can't tell". Then the 27B's legs of X1 and X4 need your call. Do not substitute L28.

**H-token** (at the predicted layer, chat rendering, primary set):
- W_E's lookup excess ≥ 0.20 while J++ leaves the lookup "around" means "around" includes token identity, which the output-side lens misses.
- W_E's lookup "around" too means the lookup's variable is neither the word form nor the token identity.
- Else mixed. W_U is reported the same way; Zach's knock-out expects it to be inert.

**Specificity** (reported; feeds "any token-aligned part would do"): the recall's excess for W_U, W_E and random next to J++'s, at every layer.
