## How to read

- *Bridge*: the hidden middle step of a two-hop question. *Answer*: what the model should say. *Distractor*: a wrong word of the bridge's kind, used as a control.
  Example: "The language spoken in the country where the Amazon River ends is": bridge "Brazil", distractor "Mexico", answer "Portuguese".
- *Cue word*: a word of the prompt that points to the bridge ("Lima" points to "Peru").
- *Last word*: the prompt's last token.
- *Numbers*: 19/63 = 19 of 63 questions; [20-42%] = 95% CI.
- *Limits*: what stops the claim from generalizing.

## A1. exp10: lenses vs logit lens, layers 32-48

- **Claim**: At layers 32-48, J++ has the bridge in its top 10 in about 6 of 10 questions, the J-lens in about 3 of 10, the logit lens almost never.
- **Setup**:
  - *Questions*: 63 lens-eval two-hop questions with a one-token word bridge ("The number of legs on the animal that spins webs is": bridge "spider").
  - *Read*: last word, each of layers 32-48, top 10 of the whole vocabulary.
  - *Score*: found if in the top 10 at any one of these layers.
  - *Control*: logit lens.
- **Result**:
  - J-lens: 19/63 = 30% [20-42%].
  - J++: 37/63 = 59% [46-70%].
  - Logit lens: 2/63 = 3% [1-11%].
  - Only the 44 the model answers correctly: 12/44, 26/44, 1/44. Same picture.
- **Limits**:
  - *Model*: Qwen3.6-27B, one run.
  - *Data*: 63 questions, one set (lens-eval). Brew and other sets not tested.
  - *Words*: one-token word bridges. Numbers, letters, multi-token bridges not tested.
  - *Control*: no distractor; a lens that ranks every word of the bridge's kind high would also pass (A2 adds one).
  - *Measure*: tolerance – one layer in the top 10 is enough.

## A3. exp12: when the last word misses the bridge, a cue word often has it

- **Claim**: Where the J-lens misses the bridge at the last word, it often finds it at a cue word. The bridge is readable there; this does not show the model computed it.
- **Setup**:
  - *Questions*: the 77 of A2.
  - *Read*: every word of the prompt, layers 32-48, among the 97 entities.
  - *Score*: seen at a word if ranked first at half or more of layers 32-48. If the last word misses, search all earlier words.
  - *Cue words*: one span per question (`data/cue_spans.json`, written by the coding agent).
  - *Control*: the same search for the distractor (many words give many chances).
- **Result**:
  - J-lens, 47/77: seen at the last word (distractor 3/77).
  - J-lens, 30/77: missed there. Of these:
    - 22/30 = 73% [56-86%]: seen at an earlier word, all 22 at a cue word.
    - 8/30: not seen anywhere.
  - Same 30, earlier words: bridge only 17, distractor only 5, both 5, neither 3. Bridge 40 points higher [13-67].
  - By category: 17 of 18 misses recovered in the small categories, 5 of 12 in "multihop".
  - J++: 46/77 at the last word (distractor 3). Of the other 31: 24 at an earlier word (23 at a cue word), 7 nowhere. Bridge only 18, distractor only 2, both 6, neither 5; 52 points higher [29-74].
- **Limits**:
  - *Model*: Qwen3.6-27B, one run.
  - *Data*: 77 questions, one set (probe-swap). Recovery comes almost all from the small categories (1-6 questions each); the 7 "multihop" misses that stay unseen have no word naming the bridge (a rhyme, a Roman god, a birthstone). Single categories are too small to judge. Brew and other sets not tested.
  - *Words*: one-token bridges.
  - *Control*: cannot tell a cue word's own association ("Lima" points to "Peru") from a computed bridge. Cue words are about half of the earlier words, and the distractor, when found, is at a cue word in 7 of 10.
  - *Measure*: one cut-off (half of layers 32-48). Shows where the bridge is readable, not where it is used.

## A4. exp14: on the cue phrase, the bridge swap changes the answer even with the answer held

- **Claim**: On the cue phrase, swapping the bridge changes the model's answer even when the answer's coordinates are held; swapping the answer with the bridge held almost never does. Clear for J++, weaker for the J-lens.
- **Setup**:
  - *Questions*: the 72 of A2's 77 with a one-token answer (both swaps possible).
  - *Edit*: cue phrase ("Amazon River ends"), layers 24-40. Bridge swap: exchange the "Brazil"/"Mexico" coordinates along the lens directions, with "Portuguese"/"Spanish" held at clean values. Answer swap: the reverse.
  - *Score*: changed if the greedy continuation gives the new answer ("Spanish") and not the clean one.
  - *Control*: random direction, same words and layers, edit 4-5x larger (median). Decision rule fixed before the run.
- **Result**:
  - J-lens: bridge swap 13/72 = 18% [11-28%], answer swap 4/72 = 6% [2-13%], random 0/72. No question changes under both.
  - J++: bridge swap 13/72 = 18% [11-28%], answer swap 1/72 = 1% [0-7%], random 0/72. No question changes under both.
  - Clean answer lost without the new one: 2/72 (J-lens), 3/72 (J++), all number answers.
  - By bridge (J-lens): places 12/25 = 48% [30-67%], people 0/6, other 1/41. J++: 10/25 = 40% [23-59%], 1/6, 2/41.
- **Limits**:
  - *Model*: Qwen3.6-27B, one run.
  - *Data*: 72 questions, one set (probe-swap). Almost all of the effect is on place bridges (countries, cities); 6 people are too few to judge; most categories have 1-6 questions. Brew and other sets not tested.
  - *Words*: one-token bridges and answers.
  - *Control*:
    - The answer swap misses its own bar, set before the run: at the last word (layers 40-56) it changes 17% (J++) and 26% (J-lens), below 30%. So its near-zero rate on the cue phrase does not show the answer is absent there.
    - Its edit is about 0.8x the bridge swap's (median per question).
    - J-lens 13 vs 4 is borderline; J++ 13 vs 1 is not.
  - *Measure*: most questions change under neither swap (58/72 J++, 55/72 J-lens). Only the lens directions tested.
