Why (Dan): so far the pointer directions are necessary, and sufficient when taken from each scene's own change. Neither shows that they hold an address the model reads by. This batch writes one generic address, the same vector for every scene, and asks whether the lookup goes there.

Scenes: the routes test's original prompts O from its C2 items, at each model's layer fixed in advance (9B L12, 27B L32, Muse L26; the 122B L33 at gate 0.75, after the data, as its earlier routes runs); the same eligibility and gate, no new scenes. The bridge item's locker sentence is at slot j in {0, 1, 2}.

Address vectors: mu_0..mu_4, the layout-free class means behind the pointer directions (the class means of the locker sentence's index after each layout group's mean is subtracted), from the existing fit, not refit; mu_k - mu_j lies in the pointer span by construction.

Arms, all written at the bridge word at the layer fixed in advance (nothing from any other prompt):
- write(j -> k, s): h + s (mu_k - mu_j), for both k != j in {0, 1, 2} (2 writes per scene); s = 1 primary, s = 2 and 4 descriptive.
- random(s): h + a random direction with the norm of s (mu_k - mu_j), one per write, seed fixed, for s = 1 and 4.
- halfway (descriptive), s = 1 and 4: scenes with j = 0, h + s (mu_2 - mu_0) / 2; scenes with j = 2, h + s (mu_0 - mu_2) / 2.

Read: X0's chat read, both readers. Lookup answers: target (locker k's digit), original (locker j's), third locker, other. Recall answers: changed or not.

Reported: per model and s, the target / original / third / other shares, overall and for each of the 6 (j, k) pairs, and the same for random; the share of writes where the recall changed; ||mu_k - mu_j|| against the median ||U U^T Delta|| of the real C2 changes (where s = 1 sits). Cluster bootstrap over bridge items, 10,000 resamples, seed 0.

Bar check (the rule since ~12:55; these are share differences, not extra losses): a random direction of the same norm almost never lands on locker k, so the ceiling of target minus random is about 1; the bars 0.15 and 0.10 are within reach.

Pre-stated readings (Dan):
- Target well above random and the third locker rare: the pointer directions hold an address the lookup reads, whatever the item (a pointer in the strict sense).
- The lookup moves, but to the third locker about as often as to the target: the directions disrupt the read; they do not address it.
- Nothing at s = 1, hits at s = 2-4: the address is there, but the average move is weaker than a real change. Descriptive, not a claim.
- The recall changes in more than 0.05 of writes: the write is not lookup-specific.
- Halfway writes land mostly on the middle locker (slot 1): an ordinal code (a line). Mostly on an end, or nowhere: a categorical code.
- Caveat for the write-up: adding a direction is where subspace illusions are most likely (Makelov et al. 2023). Against one here: mu_k - mu_j lies in the span the states actually vary along, and the target-vs-third split checks specificity.
