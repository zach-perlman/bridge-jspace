**Slide 1's speaker text (the joint story).** A two-hop question passes through one word, the bridge. The model can answer it two ways: recall the bridge entity's fact from memory, or look up a fact the prompt states. The J++ lens reads something at the bridge token in every model we tried. What it reads is which entity the bridge is — a context-free pointer to the entity, not its fact. The recall runs through that pointer: remove it (over a band, or from a single-layer patch) and recall breaks while lookup stays; swap in another entity's and the model recalls the other entity. The lookup runs around it, on an address — where the entity's sentence sits in the prompt — that a probe reads at 0.92–0.99, a swap moves, and the lens cannot see. And readable is not used: at the last token the lens still reads the entity at 0.95+, but the answer no longer goes through it; across nine models the entity is readable everywhere and needed in three. For a J++-based monitor this means: at the bridge it will tell you which entity the model is about to recall from; it will not tell you whether the model is recalling or looking up, and at the output it cannot tell whether what it reads is in use.
