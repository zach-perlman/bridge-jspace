# Prior work

Our working claims set next to the papers closest to them. The claims are not verified results; their status is in the [README](../README.md). Each paper was checked on 23 Oct 2026 against its own text: authors and arXiv id from its arXiv page, venue from the conference or proceedings page, and each summary written after reading the abstract and the section that bears on our claim.

## 1. A lookup ends with a late attention read of the answer's sentence

Our claim: the tokens after the question read the answer's sentence late, through attention, and take its digit, while the question's tokens take the entity's name (an address and a payload, as in a lookback). On hybrid models the read runs through the attention layers, not the DeltaNet layers.

| Paper | What it shows | Our claim against it |
|---|---|---|
| [Wu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15574), "Retrieval Head Mechanistically Explains Long-Context Factuality", ICLR 2025, arXiv:2404.15574 | In LLaMA, Yi, Qwen and Mistral models, fewer than 5% of attention heads copy a planted fact out of a long context; masking them, but not random heads, breaks retrieval and causes hallucination, and the authors know of no linear-attention or state-space model that passes the same test. | Agrees that a lookup ends in an attention read; we block by token pairs in every attention layer, so whether a few heads carry it is not tested here. |
| [Prakash et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14685), "Language Models Use Lookbacks to Track Beliefs", ICLR 2026, arXiv:2505.14685 | In belief-tracking stories (Llama-3-70B and others), a token holds an address next to a payload, a later token holds a matching pointer that serves as the attention query, and the match copies the payload forward; they call this a lookback. | Agrees in shape: the name is matched at the question and the digit is fetched by the last tokens, like an address and then a payload; extends it to lookups in a list and to hybrid models. |
| [Bick, Xing & Gu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18574), "Understanding the Skill Gap in Recurrent Language Models: The Role of the Gather-and-Aggregate Mechanism", ICML 2025 (PMLR 267), arXiv:2504.18574 | Transformers and state-space models both retrieve from context through a few "gather" and "aggregate" heads; state-space layers do it poorly because their mixing is too smooth, and pretrained hybrids leave the aggregate step to their attention layers. | Agrees: in our hybrids the read also runs through attention; extends it from state-space hybrids to Gated DeltaNet hybrids. |
| [Arora et al. 2024a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927), "Zoology: Measuring and Improving Recall in Efficient Language Models", ICLR 2024, arXiv:2312.04927 | Across 17 pretrained models, attention-free gated-convolution models (H3, Hyena, RWKV, RetNet) trail attention mostly on recalling tokens seen earlier in the context; multi-query associative recall (MQAR) isolates the gap, and hybrids with a little input-dependent attention close most of it. | Agrees with that division of labor; we show where trained hybrids route the read, not that their DeltaNet layers could not do it. |
| [Arora et al. 2024b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18668), "Simple linear attention language models balance the recall-throughput tradeoff", ICML 2024, arXiv:2402.18668 | A recurrent model's recall falls with its state size; pairing linear attention with sliding-window attention trades memory against recall along that curve. | Agrees: a fixed-size state is the weak reader, which fits a hybrid keeping exact reads in its attention layers. |
| [Yang, Kautz & Hatamizadeh 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464), "Gated Delta Networks: Improving Mamba2 with Delta Rule", ICLR 2025, arXiv:2412.06464 | Introduces the Gated DeltaNet layer (the linear-attention layer in our hybrids), whose gated delta-rule memory improves in-context retrieval over Mamba2 and DeltaNet. | Differs in emphasis: the layer is built for retrieval, yet blocking the answer's sentence in those layers leaves our lookups intact. |

## 2. Which sentence is read: an order label, then the entity's identity

Our claim: an order label picks the 1st and 2nd sentence; the entity's identity picks the rest.

| Paper | What it shows | Our claim against it |
|---|---|---|
| [Feng & Steinhardt 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191), "How do Language Models Bind Entities in Context?", ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.17191 | Pythia and LLaMA models tie each entity to its attribute with an abstract binding ID, indexed by the pair's order and independent of its content, and look attributes up by that ID rather than by token position; shown with 2 or 3 pairs. | Agrees for the 1st and 2nd sentence; in our longer lists identity, not an order code, picks the later ones, which their 2-3 pair setups do not reach. |
| [Dai, Heinzerling & Inui 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05448), "Representational Analysis of Binding in Language Models", EMNLP 2024 (2024.emnlp-main.967), arXiv:2409.05448 | PCA on entity activations finds a low-rank "ordering ID" subspace; pushing the query entity along it moves the answer roughly down a 7-box list (Llama-2/3, Qwen1.5, Pythia). | Differs past the 2nd item: their order code steers among 7 boxes, while here identity decides after the 2nd sentence (they push the query entity; we patch the last tokens). |
| [Prakash et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14685), "Language Models Use Lookbacks to Track Beliefs", ICLR 2026, arXiv:2505.14685 | Its lookbacks key on ordering IDs that mark a character, object or state as first or second in the story. | Agrees for the 1st and 2nd item; their stories hold two of each, so later items are not tested there. |
| [Gur-Arieh, Geva & Geiger 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06182), "Mixing Mechanisms: How Language Models Retrieve Bound Entities In-Context", ICLR 2026, arXiv:2510.06182 | Across nine models and ten tasks, retrieving a bound entity mixes a positional mechanism, reliable only at the first and last positions (a U-shape), with a lexical one (through the bound partner) and a reflexive one (a direct pointer) that take over in the middle as lists grow. | Agrees at the start and in the middle; differs at the end: we see no last-position peak for the order label. |

## 3. A band of layers where the lookup ignores the lens-readable form

Our claim: in a band of layers, the lookup ignores the J-lens-readable form of a stated entity, while recall from memory uses it.

| Paper | What it shows | Our claim against it |
|---|---|---|
| [Gurnee et al. 2026](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html), "Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models", Transformer Circuits Thread, 6 Jul 2026 | Fits a Jacobian lens (J-lens) that reads what a hidden state is poised to put into words, calls the subspace it reads the J-space, and shows on Claude models that it carries multi-step internal reasoning (swapping a two-hop question's intermediate there flips the answer) while automatic work such as continuing text, extracting spans or one-step factual recall largely bypasses it; it plays this role from about a third of the way through the layers to shortly before the output. | Agrees and narrows it: recall through a bridge uses the lens-readable form, while looking up a stated bridge, close to their span extraction, ignores it in a band of layers; tested on open models. |
| [Biran et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12775), "Hopping Too Late: Exploring the Limitations of Large Language Models on Multi-Hop Queries", EMNLP 2024, arXiv:2406.12775 | In two-hop recall questions the bridge entity is resolved in early layers and the second hop starts only after it, in later layers; copying later hidden states back to earlier layers fixes up to 66% of previously wrong answers. | Consistent: recall's second hop uses the resolved bridge later in depth; we add that, in a band of layers, a lookup of a stated bridge does not use its lens-readable form while recall does. |

**Not verified, dropped:** none of the candidate papers. Left out because sources disagree or could not be tied to one version: whether Wu et al. was an ICLR oral (its conference pages say both oral and poster), the layer ranges of Prakash et al.'s lookbacks (the model behind each range is unclear in the text), and the size of Arora et al. 2024b's recall gain (6.22 or 10.36 points, by version).
